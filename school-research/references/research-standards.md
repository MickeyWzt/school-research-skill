# Single-School Research Standards

## Source Priority

Use sources in this order:

1. Official school pages: admissions, program or department, financial aid, international office, registrar, housing, and catalog pages.
2. Official PDFs and data releases: Common Data Set, prospectus, course catalog, cost of attendance, scholarship rules, and policy documents.
3. Government, accreditation, and testing bodies when relevant.
4. Rankings, third-party databases, forums, blogs, consultants, and student reviews as secondary context only.

When sources disagree, prefer the most direct official page. If two official pages disagree, report the conflict and include both links.

## Minimum Deep-Research Source Set

For a deep profile, attempt all of these source categories before finalizing:

1. Main school profile: official about page, facts page, or school profile.
2. Admissions: official admissions requirements, application process, deadlines, and applicant category pages.
3. Program or academics: department, major, curriculum, course catalog, academic bulletin, or program handbook.
4. Cost and aid: tuition, cost of attendance, financial aid, scholarships, and international aid eligibility when relevant.
5. International or applicant support: international admissions, visa/I-20/CAS guidance, English requirements, advising, or student services.
6. Student life and housing: housing, campus life, clubs, safety, health, dining, transport, or residential life.
7. Outcomes and reputation evidence: Common Data Set, official outcomes report, career services report, graduation/retention data, or official annual report.
8. External context: accreditation, government data, rankings, student reviews, or local context, used only after official sources.

If a source category is unavailable, say `not found in this pass` and explain the decision risk. Do not silently omit the category.

## Current-Information Rules

Admissions requirements, deadlines, test policies, tuition, scholarship availability, visa rules, and program availability can change. Browse current sources for these facts unless the user asks not to. Include accessed dates in source logs.

When a page is undated, state that it is undated and avoid overclaiming freshness. For deadlines, include the exact application cycle when available.

## Evidence Labels

Use these labels when facts are uncertain:

- `verified`: Directly supported by an official or highly reliable source.
- `inferred`: Reasonable conclusion from multiple sources, but not directly stated.
- `unclear`: Evidence is missing, conflicting, outdated, or too broad.

## Fit Analysis

Assess fit across five dimensions:

- Academic fit: major, curriculum, prerequisites, faculty or department strength, research or practical opportunities.
- Admissions competitiveness: published admit rates, academic profile, required materials, selectivity signals, and student profile alignment.
- Financial fit: tuition, living costs, aid eligibility, scholarship competitiveness, and currency or funding risks.
- Environment fit: location, campus size, housing, culture, climate, safety, support for international students, and career ecosystem.
- Strategic value: application workload, deadline timing, essay reuse, portfolio needs, interview risk, and whether the school fills a portfolio role.

Use reach/target/likely only as a planning label, not a promise.

## Single-School Depth Checklist

For a named school, try to cover:

- Identity: official name, campus, location, school type, level served, and relevant college or department.
- Program: intended major, curriculum, course structure, prerequisites, concentrations, research or practical opportunities.
- Admissions: applicant category, application platform, materials, tests, English proficiency, recommendations, essays, interviews, portfolio or audition, deadlines, and decision rounds.
- Cost: tuition, fees, living costs, housing, insurance, aid eligibility, scholarships, and deposit requirements.
- Outcomes: graduation, placement, career services, alumni or graduate-school pathways when available.
- Environment: housing, campus culture, location, safety, transportation, international-student support, clubs, and advising.
- Risks: unclear policies, inconsistent pages, high cost, low aid availability, demanding prerequisites, deadline compression, or weak program match.
- Next actions: emails to admissions or department, documents to prepare, questions for campus visit, and application timeline.

## Minimum Final Report Standard

A deep report should include:

- A bottom-line recommendation with confidence level.
- A factual school identity block.
- Admissions requirements and deadline table.
- Program or curriculum analysis for the user's intended path.
- Cost and aid analysis with international-student caveats when relevant.
- Student environment and support analysis.
- Outcomes or pathway evidence.
- Applicant-fit assessment when student background is available.
- Risk register with severity and mitigation.
- Source log with accessed dates.
- Next-action checklist.

If the user asks for a quick answer, provide a shorter version but preserve citations, uncertainty labels, and next actions.

## Safety and Boundaries

Do not present school research as legal, immigration, financial, or guaranteed admissions advice. For visa, tax, or legal issues, summarize official guidance and recommend checking the relevant authority or a qualified adviser.
